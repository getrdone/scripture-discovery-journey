# Proposed Multi-Agent Benchmark Prompt

Create a complete pre-build content and experience package for a curiosity-driven Scripture journey centered on this question:

> Why did Jesus ask Peter, “Do you love me?” three times in John 21:15–19?

The primary audience is an intelligent adult who knows Peter denied Jesus three times but may not know the literary, historical, pastoral, or theological context of John 21. Use the NRSVue for Scripture quotations and references. Keep quotations brief and accurate. Do not invent sources, consensus, historical facts, Greek-language claims, or theological certainty. Clearly distinguish textual observation, historical context, interpretation, application, and unresolved questions. If a claim cannot be verified from available material, flag it for source verification instead of presenting it as fact.

Treat this as one cross-channel project with one governing promise. Make and lock reasoned planning selections where needed, including a recommended title and package, so the result is internally coherent. Do not claim that the project or user has approved those selections.

Deliver the package as planning documents under `tests/results/<agent>/peter-restoration/`, where `<agent>` is the name supplied at launch. Include:

1. `project-brief.md` — the canonical brief, current truthful status, audience, core question, why it matters, known anchor, new idea, promise, primary payoff, evidence plan, truth boundaries, tone, learning path, locked decisions, open decisions, and next curiosity.
2. `content-plan.md` — a Scripture-respecting evidence path through John 21:15–19 and essential surrounding context; the Attention → Emotion → Clarity → Progress → Payoff → Reflection → Next Curiosity sequence; early and primary payoffs; honest interpretive boundaries; plain-language sample copy for the opening, primary answer, reflection, and one “Go Deeper” route plus four alternate next paths.
3. `youtube-plan.md` — audience and channel role; idea versus packaging distinction; a full applicable title-category exploration; evaluation and a selected recommended title; 3–4 complementary thumbnail concepts with no more than four words of overlay text and one marked for first testing; a 95–105-word main YouTube description; and a video structure with an 8–25-second opening, nested Promise → Progress → Payoff cycles, visual evidence, retention beats that do not delay value, and a resolved ending.
4. `visual-direction.md` — a content-derived visual DNA, learning-focused Gestalt structure, focal point, eye path, composition, grayscale hierarchy, typography and palette direction, imagery, thumbnail/feed behavior, interaction style, purposeful motion and reduced-motion behavior, an avoid list, and a design fingerprint that avoids generic template repetition.
5. `interaction-plan.md` — optional progressive enhancements that help comparison, context, reflection, or orientation without grading the learner; keyboard, touch, focus, no-JavaScript, and reduced-motion behavior; and a clear account of what remains available in semantic HTML.
6. `web-plan.md` — semantic page outline and source order only, not production HTML; visible answer-first content; landmarks and heading strategy; accessibility requirements; performance budget and asset strategy; metadata recommendations; crawlable evidence and citations; accurate JSON-LD recommendations only if supported by visible content; SEO/AEO/GEO query-and-entity plan; and manual plus automated validation plans.
7. `quality-gates.md` — run the applicable integrity, planning, learning/content, visual, YouTube, technical-web-planning, and experience gates. Record each as PASS, FAIL, or BLOCKED with concise evidence and the next correction. Do not hide unresolved dependencies.

Use short, concrete language suited to adults at roughly a 3rd–5th-grade reading level without sounding childish. Preserve learner dignity. Do not use fear, guilt, shame, pressure, sensationalism, manipulative suspense, an unnamed teacher “we,” or a conclusion-first proof-texting approach. The promised answer and primary navigation must never depend on JavaScript.

This benchmark authorizes planning only. The project is not `approved-for-build`. Do not create production HTML, CSS, JavaScript, generated images, or final media assets. End with the truthful current project status, the exact decisions that still require explicit human approval, and a clear statement that production can begin only after the content, visual, and interaction plans are explicitly approved and the status is changed to `approved-for-build`.
