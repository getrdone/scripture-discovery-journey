# Content plan — Hell Scripture Journey (candidate)

**Method:** Scripture compared with Scripture. No denominational labels. Plain teaching voice.

## Core question (one sentence)
What does the Bible itself say about the final fate of the wicked—and does it teach never-ending conscious torment?

## Audience
Adult reader familiar with traditional language about hell who wants the Bible’s own picture.

## Promise
A clear, evidence-first walk through key passages so the learner can see the Bible’s teaching for themselves.

## Learning patterns (selected)
- LP-GU-01 (promise confirmation)
- LP-GU-02 (familiar anchor → new distinction)
- LP-AD-03 (side-by-side passage comparison)
- LP-AA-04 (orientation / small payoffs)
- LP-AA-05 (close + next curiosity)

## Evidence sequence (payoff ladder)

### 1. Promise confirmation
Name the question. State that only the Bible’s own words and context will be used.

### 2. Familiar anchor → first distinction
Many people hear “hell” and picture endless conscious suffering. The Bible’s first and last word on the subject is *death*.

- Romans 6:23 — “the wages of sin is death; but the gift of God is eternal life through Jesus Christ our Lord.”
- John 3:16 — “should not perish, but have everlasting life.”

Observation: the opposite of everlasting life is perishing / death, not everlasting life in torment.

### 3. Nature of man and the state of the dead
- Genesis 2:7 — man became a living soul (nephesh) when God breathed the breath of life into the dust.
- Genesis 3:19 — “dust thou art, and unto dust shalt thou return.”
- Ecclesiastes 9:5–6, 10 — “the dead know not any thing… neither have they any more a reward… there is no work, nor device, nor knowledge, nor wisdom, in the grave, whither thou goest.”
- Psalm 146:4 — “His breath goeth forth, he returneth to his earth; in that very day his thoughts perish.”

Observation: the dead are not conscious in intermediate torment or bliss; thoughts cease.

### 4. The soul that sins dies
- Ezekiel 18:4, 20 — “the soul that sinneth, it shall die.”
- Matthew 10:28 — “fear him which is able to destroy both soul and body in hell.”

Observation: the soul is not presented as inherently immortal; it can be destroyed.

### 5. The day that burns as an oven
- Malachi 4:1–3 — “the day that cometh shall burn as an oven… all the proud… shall be stubble… it shall leave them neither root nor branch… ye shall tread down the wicked; for they shall be ashes under the soles of your feet.”

Observation: the wicked become ashes. Nothing remains.

### 6. Everlasting destruction and the second death
- 2 Thessalonians 1:9 — “who shall be punished with everlasting destruction from the presence of the Lord.”
- Revelation 20:14–15 — “death and hell were cast into the lake of fire. This is the second death.”
- Revelation 21:8 — “the lake which burneth with fire and brimstone: which is the second death.”

Observation: the final end is called the second death. Destruction is everlasting in its result, not in ongoing conscious process.

### 7. “Eternal fire” defined by the Bible’s own examples
- Jude 7 — Sodom and Gomorrha “are set forth for an example, suffering the vengeance of eternal fire.”
- 2 Peter 2:6 — God “turning the cities of Sodom and Gomorrha into ashes condemned them with an overthrow, making them an ensample unto those that after should live ungodly.”

Observation: the fire is called eternal, yet the cities are ashes and the people are gone. The result is permanent; the process was not endless conscious torment of living beings.

### 8. Primary answer (plain language)
The consistent testimony of Scripture is that the wages of sin is death. The wicked are destroyed, reduced to ashes, and experience the second death. The Bible does not teach that the wicked live forever in conscious torment. Eternal life is the gift given only to those who receive it through Christ; the alternative is perishing.

### 9. Boundaries and remaining questions
- Parables (such as Luke 16) use figurative language and must not override the plain didactic teaching on the state of the dead and the final judgment.
- Words translated “hell” (Sheol, Hades, Gehenna, Tartarus) require careful contextual study; this journey focuses on the final fate rather than intermediate state vocabulary.
- The exact experience of the lost at the final judgment is described with consuming fire and final death; details beyond the text remain open.

### 10. Reflection and next curiosity
Featured: What does the Bible teach about the state of the dead before the resurrection?

Alternates:
- What is the second death in Revelation?
- How does the Bible use the word “hell”?
- What does the Bible say about the immortality of the soul?
- How does the Bible describe the new earth and the end of death?
