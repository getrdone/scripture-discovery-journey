What does the Bible say about hell?

This study asks one question and follows the Bible's own words from beginning to end. It does not assume that every traditional picture of hell is correct. It also does not deny judgment, fire, or punishment. The question is what those things finally do.

Does the Bible teach that the wicked remain alive forever in conscious torment, or that sin finally ends in death and destruction?

Start with the clearest contrast

Before looking at symbolic passages, begin with the Bible's simplest words. Romans 6:23 says the wages of sin is death, while the gift of God is eternal life. John 3:16 places perishing opposite everlasting life.

That gives a starting principle: eternal life is God's gift. The alternative is death and perishing.

1 · What is man?

Life is given by God

Genesis 2:7 · Genesis 3:19 · Ecclesiastes 12:7

Genesis says God formed man from the dust and breathed into him the breath of life, and man became a living soul. At death, the body returns to dust and the life God gave returns to Him.

The Bible begins with life as something received from God, not something naturally indestructible.

2 · What happens in death?

The dead are described as unconscious

Ecclesiastes 9:5–6, 10 · Psalm 146:4 · Psalm 115:17 · John 11:11–14

Ecclesiastes says the dead know nothing. Psalm 146 says a person's thoughts perish when his breath goes forth. Jesus repeatedly used sleep as a picture of death while pointing toward resurrection.

The ordinary biblical picture of death is not conscious life continuing somewhere else. It is a state from which God raises the dead.

3 · Can a soul die?

Scripture says it can

Ezekiel 18:4, 20 · Matthew 10:28

Ezekiel says, "the soul that sinneth, it shall die." Jesus says God is able to destroy both soul and body in Gehenna.

Whatever else a person may believe about the soul, these passages do not describe it as something God cannot destroy.

4 · How does the Bible describe the final result?

Perish. Consume. Stubble. Ashes.

Psalm 37:9–10, 20, 38 · Obadiah 16 · Malachi 4:1–3

Psalm 37 says the wicked perish and consume away. Obadiah says they become as though they had not been. Malachi says the wicked are burned as stubble and become ashes beneath the feet of the righteous.

These are pictures of destruction completed, not life preserved forever in pain.

5 · Difficult passage

What does “unquenchable fire” mean?

Matthew 3:12 · Mark 9:43–48 · Jeremiah 17:27 · Isaiah 66:24

Jeremiah warned that Jerusalem would burn with a fire that would not be quenched. Jerusalem is not still burning. An unquenchable fire is one that cannot be stopped before it finishes its work.

What about “their worm dieth not”?

Jesus' wording comes from Isaiah 66:24, where the scene is the carcasses of rebels. The worm and fire describe complete disgrace and destruction of the dead bodies.

6 · Difficult passage

What does “eternal fire” mean?

Jude 7 · 2 Peter 2:6

Jude calls the destruction of Sodom and Gomorrah an example of eternal fire. Peter explains the result: the cities were turned into ashes.

The Bible's own example shows a fire with an eternal result, not a fire that must still be burning today.

7 · Difficult passage

What about “everlasting punishment”?

Matthew 25:46 · 2 Thessalonians 1:9 · Hebrews 6:2

Matthew says the punishment is everlasting. Paul calls the fate of the lost "everlasting destruction." The duration belongs to the result of the judgment. A punishment can be everlasting because it is never reversed.

The question is not whether the punishment lasts forever. It does. The question is whether the Bible defines that punishment as everlasting life in pain or as everlasting destruction.

8 · Revelation gives it a name

The lake of fire is the second death

Revelation 20:14–15 · Revelation 21:8

Twice Revelation explains the lake of fire with the same phrase: the second death.

That inspired definition should control how the symbol is read. The lake of fire ends in death.

9 · Difficult passage

What about Revelation 20:10?

Revelation 20:10 · Revelation 19:20 · Revelation 20:14 · Ezekiel 28:18–19

Revelation 20:10 uses the strongest language in the Bible for the punishment of the devil. That text must be allowed to stand. But the same chapter still defines the lake of fire as the second death. Scripture also says Satan will finally be brought to ashes and "never shalt thou be any more" in Ezekiel's judgment language.

The passage is difficult, but it should not be isolated from Revelation's own explanation of the lake of fire or from the Bible's wider language of Satan's final destruction.

10 · Difficult passage

What about “the smoke of their torment ascendeth up for ever and ever”?

Revelation 14:9–11 · Isaiah 34:9–10

Revelation uses Old Testament judgment language. Isaiah says the smoke of Edom would go up forever, yet the prophecy describes a land left desolate rather than a region burning without end.

“Smoke ascending forever” is biblical language for a judgment whose destruction is complete and whose result does not reverse.

11 · Difficult passage

What about the rich man and Lazarus?

Luke 16:19–31 · Luke 15:3 · Luke 16:1

The story appears in a section filled with parables. Its purpose is moral warning: people who refuse Moses and the prophets will not be persuaded even by a resurrection. If every detail is treated as literal geography, the story produces several difficulties that conflict with the Bible's plain teaching about death and resurrection.

A parable should teach the point Jesus made with it. It should not overturn clear statements elsewhere about the condition of the dead.

12 · A word-study safeguard

“Hell” translates more than one biblical word

WordBasic use
SheolThe grave or realm of the dead in the Old Testament.
HadesThe New Testament term commonly corresponding to Sheol.
GehennaJesus' judgment term drawn from the Valley of Hinnom.
TartarusUsed once, in 2 Peter 2:4, for the confinement of fallen angels.

The English word “hell” can hide important differences. Each passage should be read in context.

13 · Who has immortality?

Immortality is a gift

1 Timothy 6:15–16 · Romans 2:7 · 1 Corinthians 15:51–54

God alone has immortality inherently. Human beings are told to seek immortality, and the saved put on immortality at the resurrection.

That fits the earlier contrast: eternal life belongs to those who receive God's gift. Scripture does not require the wicked to be made immortal in order to be punished.

Put the passages together

The Bible's repeated language is consistent: the wages of sin is death. The wicked perish. They are destroyed. They are consumed. They become ashes. The lake of fire is called the second death.

The difficult phrases—eternal fire, everlasting punishment, unquenchable fire, smoke ascending forever—are real and serious. But when Scripture interprets those ideas with Scripture, they describe a judgment whose result is everlasting, not an endless preservation of sinful life.

That means the final punishment of the wicked is complete and irreversible destruction. Sin and sinners finally come to an end. Eternal life remains exactly what Scripture calls it: the gift of God through Jesus Christ.
