# Project Brief — What Does the Bible Really Say About Hell?

status: approved-for-build

## Governing question
Does the Bible teach that the lost remain alive in conscious torment forever, or that sin finally ends in death and destruction?

## Audience
Adult Bible readers who have heard the common idea of everlasting conscious torment and want to compare the passages for themselves.

## Promise
Trace the subject from Eden through Jesus, the apostles, and Revelation. Define death, immortality, hell/fire language, judgment, and the second death by comparing Scripture with Scripture.

## Public voice
Scripture-first. Do not mention Adventism, Adventists, denominational identity, Ellen White, pioneers, or institutional authority in customer-facing copy. Do not attack people or churches. Present the biblical evidence calmly and let the reader compare texts.

## Internal theological alignment
Conditional immortality / final destruction of the wicked. The wicked are not naturally immortal; the dead await resurrection; final punishment culminates in the second death. Internal research may use approved sources and denominational materials to check coverage, but Scripture must carry the public argument.

## Core evidence path
1. Genesis 2:7, 16–17; 3:4, 19, 22–24 — life, death, and the tree of life.
2. Ezekiel 18:4, 20; Romans 6:23 — the stated wages of sin.
3. 1 Timothy 6:15–16; 1 Corinthians 15:51–54 — immortality belongs to God inherently and is bestowed on the redeemed.
4. Ecclesiastes 9:5–6, 10; Psalm 146:4; John 11:11–14 — death described as unconscious sleep/rest.
5. John 5:28–29; 1 Thessalonians 4:13–17 — resurrection, rather than an immortal soul, is the hope presented to the dead.
6. Matthew 10:28; John 3:16; Matthew 7:13 — perish, destroy, life, death.
7. Matthew 13:40–42, 49–50; 25:41 — final fire and judgment.
8. Malachi 4:1–3; Psalm 37:9–10, 20, 38; Obadiah 16 — consumed, no more, ashes, as though they had not been.
9. Jude 7 with Genesis 19 and 2 Peter 2:6 — “eternal fire” tested by its biblical example: Sodom’s irreversible result, not a city still burning.
10. Revelation 20:5–15; 21:4, 8 — resurrection of the lost, lake of fire, second death, and the end of death itself.

## Difficult passages to address fairly
Mark 9:43–48; Matthew 25:46; Luke 16:19–31; Revelation 14:9–11; Revelation 20:10; Isaiah 66:24. Do not dodge these. Read each in genre and context and compare its language with the rest of Scripture.
